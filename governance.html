Committee & Governance

Open meetings, published agendas and minutes, and the policies that keep our troop accountable to the families it serves.

How We're Run

Transparent, family-led Scouting

Troop 54 is run by a volunteer committee of parents and leaders, on behalf of the Scouts and families we serve. Our committee meets monthly, and every meeting is open to all Troop 54 families. In the spirit of open, accountable governance, we post our agendas ahead of each meeting and our minutes afterward, right here.

A note on privacy. To protect our youth and families, sensitive matters — anything about an individual Scout, a family's finances, or a personnel or Youth Protection concern — are handled in executive session. Those items appear in the public record only as "discussed in executive session," and we never name an individual Scout in a public document.

Financial Controls

Our financial controls policy covers budgeting, bank accounts, bill payment, cash handling, financial reporting, and annual review. It exists to keep the troop's money safe, accountable, and separate from any individual. Adopted 2025.

First meeting of the 2026–27 program year. 7:00–8:00 PM.

  1. Welcome. Introductions, and the test we use all year: is this ours, or is it the Scouts'? If it's theirs, we ask what they need.

  5. Financial. The financial controls policy requires an annual budget; the Treasurer and finance help to build one for the October meeting.
  6. Supporting Our Pack. Where the troop and Cub Pack overlap and volunteer, including Den Chief roles.
  7. Decisions. Adopt the committee meeting schedule, confirm the September new-member night, and add key deadlines (recharter, membership, popcorn) to the calendar.
  8. Open Discussion.

Scout-Led Program & the PLC

The committee affirmed the troop's move to a fully Scout-led model. The Patrol Leaders' Council, with adult guidance, will plan meetings and events. The Senior Patrol Leader will lead PLC planning for the September 21 meeting (and possibly September 28).

Program Calendar

Reviewed the year's outings, including the Harvest Feast campout (Oct 2–4), fall camporees, an orienteering day (Nov 7), a Camp Wakpominee campout (Nov 20–22, focused on Tenderfoot through First Class requirements and including Arrow of Light crossovers), and a joint MLK-weekend trip to Camp Wakpominee with Troop 1011 (Jan 15–17, possibly through the 18th). Additional possibilities discussed: a USS Slater sleepover, a Fort Ticonderoga outing, and a Boston trip (Apr 1–4) with a possible USS Constitution sleepover. Adult Leadership, Succession & Training

Discussed succession planning for key roles (Chartered Organization Representative, Committee Chair, Treasurer, Advancement Coordinator, and activity coordinators) and the need for more registered adults as the troop grows and prepares to welcome female Scouts, which will require both male and female registered leaders.
